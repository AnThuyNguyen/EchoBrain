Introduction to Cell Biology

Cells are the basic structural and functional units of all living organisms. Every living thing is made of one or more cells, and all cells arise from pre-existing cells. This principle is known as Cell Theory.

Prokaryotic vs Eukaryotic Cells
Prokaryotic cells, such as bacteria, lack a membrane-bound nucleus. Their DNA floats freely in the cytoplasm in a region called the nucleoid. Eukaryotic cells, found in plants, animals, and fungi, contain a true nucleus enclosed by a nuclear envelope, along with membrane-bound organelles.

The Cell Membrane
The cell membrane (plasma membrane) is a phospholipid bilayer that surrounds every cell. It controls what enters and exits the cell, acting as a selective barrier. Proteins embedded in the membrane help transport molecules and receive signals from the environment. This model of membrane structure is called the Fluid Mosaic Model.

Mitochondria and ATP Production
Mitochondria are organelles responsible for producing ATP (adenosine triphosphate), the cell's main energy currency. Through a process called cellular respiration, glucose and oxygen are converted into ATP, carbon dioxide, and water. Mitochondria have their own DNA and are thought to have originated from ancient bacteria — a concept called endosymbiotic theory.

The Nucleus and DNA
The nucleus contains the cell's genetic material in the form of DNA. DNA is organized into chromosomes, and segments of DNA that code for proteins are called genes. Before a cell divides, DNA is copied in a process called DNA replication, ensuring each daughter cell receives a complete set of genetic instructions.

Mitosis
Mitosis is the process of cell division that produces two genetically identical daughter cells from one parent cell. It consists of four phases: prophase, metaphase, anaphase, and telophase. Mitosis is used for growth, repair, and asexual reproduction. It is distinct from meiosis, which produces sex cells with half the normal chromosome number.

Protein Synthesis
Proteins are made in a two-step process: transcription and translation. During transcription, a section of DNA is copied into messenger RNA (mRNA) inside the nucleus. The mRNA travels to a ribosome, where translation occurs: transfer RNA (tRNA) molecules bring amino acids that are assembled into a protein chain according to the mRNA sequence.

Osmosis and Diffusion
Diffusion is the movement of molecules from an area of high concentration to low concentration. Osmosis is the diffusion of water specifically across a semipermeable membrane. When a cell is placed in a hypotonic solution (lower solute concentration), water moves into the cell and it swells. In a hypertonic solution (higher solute concentration), water moves out and the cell shrinks.
